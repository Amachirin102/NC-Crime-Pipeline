What these numbers are, and what they are not

  • These are reports, not crimes. Many crimes are never reported to police, and reporting rates differ by offense and neighborhood. A change in reports can reflect a change in reporting, not in crime.
  • One offense per report. CMPD classifies each report by its single most serious offense under the FBI's NIBRS hierarchy. A report that includes both a robbery and a car theft counts once, as a robbery. Totals here will be lower than offense-level counts published elsewhere.
  • Violent means murder, rape and other forcible sex offenses, robbery, and aggravated assault (NIBRS codes 09A, 11A–11C, 120, 13A). Property means burglary, larceny/theft, motor vehicle theft, and arson (220, 23A–23H, 240, 200). Simple assault, fraud, drug and weapon offenses are excluded from both.
  • Reports CMPD later marked "unfounded" (investigated and found false or not a crime) are excluded.
  • Months are grouped by the date the report was filed, not the date the incident happened. That keeps every record in a month (the incident date is sometimes blank or approximate) but means a crime from last year that was reported this month counts this month.
  • Recent months are preliminary. Reports keep arriving and being reclassified for weeks. Months that ended less than days before the data was pulled are shown as hollow points and are left out of the year-over-year comparison.
  • History changes. CMPD revises past records. Each monthly run compares the new data against the previous run and logs every restated number, so an earlier figure can be traced to the correction that changed it.
  • Coverage. CMPD serves Charlotte and unincorporated Mecklenburg County. The six other towns in the county (Cornelius, Davidson, Huntersville, Matthews, Mint Hill, Pineville) have their own police departments, and their crimes are mostly not in this data.
